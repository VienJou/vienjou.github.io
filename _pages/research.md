# Research Overview

My research uses geospatial data science and AI to characterize flood and dam-failure hazards and the buildings and communities exposed to them. I combine observation-based flood modeling and dam-breach inundation modeling with building-use and neighborhood information to support exposure assessment and hazard preparedness.

# Current Projects 

- ***Dam-breach hazards and preparedness*** (NSF I-GUIDE Aging Dams Convergence Project): Leading the development of a physics-guided deep learning surrogate for dam-breach inundation mapping, trained on federal and state inundation catalogues covering 1,688 dams. I also host the project's biweekly meetings of hydrologists, statisticians, GIScientists, and social scientists studying downstream vulnerability and emergency preparedness.
- ***Observation-based flood-depth prediction***: Built a benchmark of USGS high-water marks from 39 U.S. flood events and a multimodal model that predicts flood peak depth ahead of the peak, evaluated on 11 held-out events including the June 2022 Montana flood ([arXiv:2611.05220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05220)).
- ***Geospatial foundation models for urban livability***: Co-developed a framework evaluating AlphaEarth, AnySat, and TerraMind embeddings for urban livability prediction where socioeconomic data are scarce (ACM SIGSPATIAL 2026).
- ***Hydrographic feature delineation***: Contributing to workflow and experimental design for nationwide multi-task hydrographic feature delineation using multiple geospatial data sources and foundation models.


# Datasets and Software

- ***Multimodal Urban Livability Evaluation Dataset and Model***: About 52,000 samples from urban areas in the Netherlands combining remote sensing imagery, digital surface models, night-light imagery, and point-of-interest text, with labels for an overall livability score and five domain scores. Released with a pretrained model on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Vinjou/Multimodal_urban_livability_evaluation_dataset) (CC BY-NC 4.0).
- ***Doctoral Research Data and Code***: Data and code supporting four doctoral studies on building-use classification and urban livability, archived at DANS ([DOI 10.17026/PT/VFTW9X](https://doi.org/10.17026/PT/VFTW9X)).
- ***USGS High-Water-Mark Flood Peak Dataset***: Benchmark pairing USGS high-water-mark surveys from 39 U.S. flood events with terrain, Earth observation embeddings, and meteorological forcing; under construction, with public release planned on completion.


# Research Areas

### Urban resilience evaluation
- ***Multi-modal Deep Learning for flood depth prediction and mapping***: Exploring how to predict flood depth according to diverse geospatial data, including time-series data. A state-of-the-art deep learning method will be proposed and evaluated.

### Urban spatial structure and inequality analysis
- ***Multi-modal Deep Learning for Urban Livability***: Developing comprehensive frameworks to evaluate and measure urban quality of life using multiple data sources including remote sensing, social media, and urban infrastructure data
- ***Building Functional Analysis***: Extracting and analyzing building use patterns from various geospatial datasets to understand urban functional diversity
- ***Urban Property Valuation***: Using machine learning and geospatial analysis to assess property values and urban development patterns

### Geospatial AI and Remote Sensing
- ***Deep Learning Applications***: Applying multi-modal deep learning techniques to urban research problems
- ***High-resolution Remote Sensing***: Processing and analyzing high-resolution satellite imagery for urban land use classification
- ***Crop Mapping***: Developing automated methods for agricultural land use mapping and monitoring

### Data Integration and Analysis
- ***Geospatial Data Integration***: Combining remote sensing, GIS, and other spatial data for comprehensive urban analysis
- ***Mixed-Use Building Classification***: Advanced methods for identifying and classifying complex urban building functions
- ***Spatial Statistics***: Applying statistical methods to understand spatial patterns and relationships in urban environments
